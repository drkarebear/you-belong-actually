Start with the problem, not the office name

I Don’t Know Who to Ask.

College has a lot of offices, and their names are not always obvious. You do not need to know the org chart. Describe what you need, then find a reasonable first stop.

You are allowed to start in the wrong place.

If an office does not handle your question, you can ask them to point you to the right one. Getting redirected is part of navigating a college system, not evidence that you should have known better.

What are you trying to solve?

Choose the closest problem. Office names vary by college, so the result gives you a type of office to look for.

Look for:

Who handles what?

A plain-language college office guide

Your college may use a slightly different name. Focus on what the office does.

One class

Instructor

Start here for: assignment directions, class policies, grades, missed work, feedback, exams, or what to focus on next.

Useful to have: the course name, assignment name, and one specific question.

Learning support

Tutoring, Writing/Math Centers, Library, or Academic Support

Start here for: understanding material, practicing skills, starting an assignment, research, citations, or study support.

You can use these services before you are failing.

Enrollment records

Admissions & Records / Enrollment Services

Start here for: adding or dropping a class, enrollment records, transcripts, registration problems, or record-related questions.

If a prerequisite is blocking registration, counseling may also be part of the answer.

Your academic path

Counseling / Academic Advising

Start here for: which classes to take, education plans, degree or certificate requirements, academic standing, and course planning.

Transfer questions: counseling or your college’s Transfer Center can help.

Aid

Financial Aid

Start here for: FAFSA or CADAA, aid status, missing documents, grants, loans, work-study, satisfactory academic progress, or how enrollment changes may affect aid.

If you are thinking about dropping a class, ask before assuming what will happen to your aid.

Money owed to the college

Business Office / Cashier / Student Accounts

Start here for: fees, payments, refunds, account balances, or payment-related holds.

The office name varies a lot by college.

Access & accommodations

Accessibility / Disability Services

Start here for: disability-related accommodations, assistive technology, testing accommodations, or questions about access.

At California Community Colleges, this office is often called DSPS or a similar name.

Technology

IT Help Desk / Online Learning Support

Start here for: login problems, passwords, Wi-Fi, Canvas access, college email, or device/technology issues.

If the issue is only inside one Canvas course, your instructor may also need to check the course settings.

Life barriers

Basic Needs Center / Student Support Programs

Start here for: food, housing, emergency grants, transportation, benefits navigation, or other practical barriers affecting school.

The right next step may be material support, not a productivity trick.

Health, safety, or serious concern

Student Health, Mental Health, Dean/Student Services, or Equity/Title IX

Start here for: health or mental-health support, harassment, discrimination, safety concerns, or a serious student-services issue.

If there is immediate danger, use emergency services or your college’s emergency procedures.

No perfect terminology required

You can ask for directions in plain language.

I am not sure whether this is the right office.

Hi, I’m trying to get help with [briefly describe the problem]. I’m not sure which office handles this. If this is not the right place, could you tell me where I should start?

If you are standing at a counter or calling:
“Hi. I’m not sure where to start. I’m trying to figure out ___.”

Make the next conversation easier

Bring just enough information.

  • Your student ID number, if the office needs to look up your record. Do not send sensitive information unless the office asks through an appropriate channel.
  • The course or section number, if the problem involves a class.
  • The exact message, hold, error, deadline, or form you are asking about. A screenshot can help when appropriate.
  • One sentence describing what you are trying to do. For example: “I’m trying to add this class,” or “I’m trying to understand why my aid is on hold.”

If two offices seem possible

Pick the one closest to the problem and start there. College processes overlap. You are not expected to know every handoff before you ask.

If you get redirected more than once

Write down the office names and what each person told you. Then you can say, “I started with ___, and they sent me here because ___.”

Knowing who to ask is a skill you can learn.

The system may be complicated. That does not mean you are bad at college. Start with the problem, find a reasonable first stop, and let the next person help you get one step closer.
