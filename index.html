You Belong, Actually.

College can make almost anyone wonder whether they are cut out for it, especially when everyone else seems to know rules nobody explained.

This is not a pep-talk site. It is a place to figure out what is happening, what it might mean, and one useful thing you can do next.

A thing worth knowing: not knowing how college works yet is different from not being capable of college. A lot of college knowledge is learned after you arrive.

College Decoder

College has a hidden curriculum.

Office hours. Add codes. Prerequisites. Rubrics. Financial aid holds. Asking for an extension. Talking to a professor. Using tutoring.

These are learned skills, not secret evidence that everyone else belongs more than you do.

Quick help

Pick one next step.

You do not need to solve the whole semester tonight.

If you are thinking, “I do not belong here”

Try replacing the big conclusion with a smaller question: What happened that made me feel this way?

I need to ask someone something A grade triggered this I feel behind everyone else

If you got a bad grade

First, find out what the grade is telling you. Was the issue missing work, misunderstanding, evidence, organization, time, or something else?

Next move: identify one thing you would change on the next assignment.

If asking for help feels scary

You do not need a perfect explanation. A useful question can be one sentence: “I am stuck on ___, and I tried ___. What should I do next?”

If everyone else seems to know what they are doing

Some students arrive already knowing college vocabulary and routines. Others learn them here. Familiarity is not the same thing as ability.

Next move: write down the one rule, term, or process that feels unclear.

If you are falling behind

Do not start with everything. Start with what is still recoverable.

What is due next? What is worth the most? What can still be submitted? Who needs to hear from me?

If you do not know who to ask

Start with the person closest to the problem. For a class question, start with the instructor. For registration, financial aid, counseling, technology, or basic needs, use the campus office that handles that area.

This matters too

Sometimes the problem is not school.

Work, food, housing, caregiving, transportation, technology, money, and health can make school harder. A real-life barrier is not a character flaw.

I need practical support.

Look for your college’s basic needs center, food resources, emergency grants, transportation help, or housing support.

I need academic support.

Try tutoring, a writing center, library help, supplemental instruction, office hours, or an instructor appointment.

I need help navigating college.

Counseling, admissions, financial aid, disability services, and student support programs can help untangle the system.

About the project

Built for the moment when doubt gets loud.

You Belong, Actually is designed for community college students, especially students who are first in their families to navigate college or who did not arrive already knowing the unwritten rules.

The goal is simple: make college feel more understandable, make asking for help easier, and help students find a next step without turning every challenge into a judgment about themselves.
