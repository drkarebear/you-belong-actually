Plain-language definitions

Open only the term you need.

Each entry gives you the short version and the move that usually matters next.

Office hoursClass & learning

What it means: time an instructor sets aside for students to ask questions, talk through a problem, or get help.

What to do with it: you do not need to be in trouble. “Can I check that I understand the assignment?” is enough.

SyllabusClass & learning

What it means: the course guide with policies, grading information, instructor contact information, assignments, and important dates.

What to do with it: check the syllabus and course site when you have a class-specific question, then ask if the answer is still unclear.

RubricClass & learning

What it means: a guide showing the criteria used to evaluate an assignment.

What to do with it: use it before you submit, not only after the grade comes back.

UnitsClass & learning

What it means: a measure of academic credit connected to a course. Unit totals are also used in degree, enrollment-status, transfer, and aid rules.

What to do with it: do not judge workload only by the number of classes. Courses can carry different unit values and different amounts of work.

Asynchronous vs. synchronousClass & learning

Asynchronous: you complete online course work without a regular live class meeting at a set time. It can still have firm weekly deadlines.

Synchronous: the class meets live at scheduled times, whether online or in person.

HybridClass & learning

What it means: a course that combines scheduled in-person or live instruction with online work.

What to do with it: read the section details carefully. “Hybrid” does not tell you by itself which days are on campus.

Short-term / late-start classClass & learning

What it means: a course that runs for fewer weeks than the full semester or starts later in the term.

What to do with it: expect a faster pace. A shorter calendar usually compresses the course; it does not automatically mean less course content.

Pass / No Pass (P/NP)Class & learning

What it means: a grading option that records a pass or no pass instead of a letter grade.

Before choosing it: check the class, major, transfer, and financial-aid rules that apply to you. Some requirements limit how P/NP can be used.

Incomplete (I)Class & learning

What it means: a temporary notation that may be available in limited circumstances when approved course work cannot be finished by the end of the term.

Important: it is not an automatic extension. Ask your instructor or Admissions & Records about eligibility, the remaining work, and the deadline.

ZTC (Zero Textbook Cost)Class & learning

What it means: a course section identified as having zero required textbook cost.

What to do with it: still read the section notes for any other required supplies, software, or course-specific fees.

OER (Open Educational Resources)Class & learning

What it means: teaching and learning materials with open licenses that allow certain kinds of reuse and sharing.

Useful distinction: OER can help make a class ZTC, but “OER” and “ZTC” do not mean exactly the same thing.

PrerequisiteEnrollment & records

What it means: a requirement you must meet before enrolling in a particular course.

If registration blocks you: counseling or Admissions & Records can usually explain what the system is checking and what options may exist.

CorequisiteEnrollment & records

What it means: a course or requirement that must usually be taken at the same time as another course, unless you have already satisfied it.

What to do with it: if one class will not enroll, check whether the system expects you to add a linked course too.

Add code / permission numberEnrollment & records

What it means: a code or permission an instructor may provide so an eligible student can add a class after regular enrollment has begun.

Important: it may not override prerequisites, deadlines, holds, or other enrollment rules.

WaitlistEnrollment & records

What it means: a list of students waiting for an opening in a full class.

What to do with it: watch your student email and enrollment system closely. A seat offer or permission to add may have a deadline.

HoldEnrollment & records

What it means: a block on a specific college action until something is resolved, such as a required document, account issue, or other process.

What to do with it: open the hold details in your student portal. The office named there is usually the best first contact.

W (Withdrawal)Enrollment & records

What it means: a transcript notation showing that you withdrew from a course after the no-notation period.

Before withdrawing: check the deadline and possible effects on academic progress, repeat limits, financial aid, transfer timing, or your education plan.

EW (Excused Withdrawal)Enrollment & records

What it means: a withdrawal notation that may be used when circumstances beyond a student’s control make completing a course impracticable.

Why it is different: California Community Colleges treat EW differently from a regular W in statewide progress calculations. The local request process can vary, so ask Admissions & Records what your college requires.

Common Course Numbering (CCN) / the “C” in C1000Enrollment & records

What it means: California Community Colleges are moving common courses to shared numbers and course information so students can recognize comparable courses more easily across colleges.

That “C” matters: in a course such as ENGL C1000, the C identifies it as a commonly numbered course in the statewide system.

GPA (Grade Point Average)Grades & standing

What it means: a numerical summary of grades from courses that carry grade points.

Useful distinction: your term GPA covers one term; your cumulative GPA combines applicable coursework across terms.

Academic notice / progress noticeGrades & standing

What it means: a status telling you that your cumulative academic or course-completion record needs attention.

You may still hear the older word “probation.” California regulations now use academic notice and progress notice. Academic notice generally relates to grades/GPA; progress notice relates to course completion patterns.

What to do with it: read the notice, meet with the campus office it names, and ask what specific conditions you need to improve.

Academic/progress pause and restartGrades & standing

What it means: newer California terms for what was previously called academic/progress dismissal and return after dismissal.

Important: “pause” is not the same as “you can never come back.” State rules preserve access to support services during a pause, and your college can explain its restart process.

MajorTransfer & planning

What it means: your main field of study, such as psychology, nursing, English, business, or computer science.

What to do with it: degree and transfer requirements can change by major, so “a class transfers” is not always the same as “this class counts for my major.”

GE (General Education)Transfer & planning

What it means: broad course requirements outside or alongside your major, often including areas such as writing, math, arts/humanities, sciences, and social sciences.

What to do with it: use the GE pattern that belongs to your actual degree and transfer plan, not a checklist you found for somebody else.

Cal-GETCTransfer & planning

What it means: the California General Education Transfer Curriculum, a statewide GE pattern for eligible California Community College students transferring to a UC or CSU.

Why you may hear different names: Cal-GETC began for new CCC students in Fall 2025, while some continuing students may still be eligible to use older patterns such as IGETC or CSU GE. A counselor can tell you which pattern applies to your record and goal.

TransferableTransfer & planning

What it means: a receiving university may accept the course for transfer credit.

Hidden distinction: transferable does not automatically mean the course satisfies the specific major, GE, or campus requirement you need. Check the articulation agreement.

ArticulationTransfer & planning

What it means: an official agreement showing how coursework at one college can satisfy requirements at another institution.

What to do with it: for California transfer planning, use current articulation information in ASSIST and review it with a counselor.

ASSISTTransfer & planning

What it means: California’s official statewide source for course transfer and articulation information among the public higher-education systems and participating independent institutions.

What to do with it: use ASSIST to see how courses may apply to transfer requirements, then use counseling to build the full plan.

ADT / AA-T / AS-TTransfer & planning

What it means: an Associate Degree for Transfer. AA-T means Associate in Arts for Transfer; AS-T means Associate in Science for Transfer.

What it can do: students who complete an eligible ADT and meet CSU requirements receive transfer advantages, including guaranteed admission to a CSU campus. It does not guarantee a particular CSU campus or major.

Student Education Plan (SEP / ed plan)Transfer & planning

What it means: a course plan built around your educational goal, usually with a counselor.

What to do with it: update it when your major, transfer target, work schedule, or other major circumstances change.

Catalog year / catalog rightsTransfer & planning

What it means: the set of college requirements that may govern your degree based on when and how you attended. Rules are not always simply “whatever the newest catalog says.”

What to do with it: if you have been in college for several years or returned after a break, ask a counselor which catalog requirements apply to you.

FAFSAMoney & aid

What it means: the Free Application for Federal Student Aid. Eligible students use it to apply for federal aid and it can also be used to determine eligibility for state and college aid.

What to do with it: submit the correct application for each aid year and respond to any follow-up requests from your financial aid office.

CADAA (California Dream Act Application)Money & aid

What it means: California’s state financial-aid application for eligible undocumented students and others who meet applicable California requirements.

Not sure whether FAFSA or CADAA applies to you? Ask your college financial aid office rather than guessing from somebody else’s situation.

SAP (Satisfactory Academic Progress)Money & aid

What it means: the academic-progress standards used to determine whether you remain eligible for certain financial aid.

Important: SAP is a financial-aid rule, not exactly the same thing as your college’s academic standing. Read your college’s SAP policy if you receive a warning, suspension, or appeal notice.

California College Promise Grant (CCPG)Money & aid

What it means: a California Community College program that can waive enrollment fees for eligible students.

You may hear an older name: BOG Fee Waiver. Also, the CCPG is not necessarily the same thing as a local college “Promise” program.

Financial aid disbursementMoney & aid

What it means: the point when approved financial-aid funds are actually released according to the college’s process.

Hidden distinction: an aid award and a disbursement are not the same moment. Check your financial aid portal for requirements and your college’s disbursement schedule.

Basic Needs CenterCampus support

What it means: a campus starting point for resources connected to needs such as food, housing, transportation, emergency support, and other essentials.

You do not have to decide whether your problem is “serious enough” before asking what support exists.

Accessibility / Disability Services (often called DSPS)Campus support

What it means: the office that works with eligible students on disability-related access and academic accommodations. The exact office name varies by college.

What to do with it: contact the office directly to learn about eligibility, documentation, and how accommodations are put in place.

EOPSCampus support

What it means: Extended Opportunity Programs and Services, a California Community College support program for eligible students facing economic and educational barriers.

What to do with it: check your college’s EOPS eligibility and application information. Services can include counseling and other academic or financial supports.

Tutoring / Learning CenterCampus support

What it means: academic support where students can work with tutors or other learning staff on course skills and assignments.

What to do with it: you do not have to wait until you are failing. Bringing one question, problem, draft, or confusing concept is enough.

CounselingCampus support

What it means: college professionals who can help with educational planning, course selection, degree and transfer requirements, and other academic decisions.

What to do with it: use counseling before a decision becomes urgent, especially when choosing a major, changing plans, or considering dropping courses.

Transfer CenterCampus support

What it means: a campus resource focused on helping students prepare for transfer to four-year institutions.

What to do with it: look for transfer workshops, university-representative appointments, application help, and transfer-planning resources.

The unwritten part

Six things college words can hide.

Knowing the vocabulary helps, but these distinctions matter even more.

“Transferable” does not mean “counts for everything.”

A course can transfer for units without satisfying the specific major or GE requirement you need.

“Online” does not always mean “whenever you want.”

Asynchronous classes can still have weekly deadlines, timed exams, required check-ins, or group work.

“Financial aid awarded” does not always mean “money sent today.”

Awarding, eligibility checks, and disbursement are different steps.

A campus office name is not a test.

You can say, “I’m not sure whether this is the right office. Can you tell me where to start?”

College vocabulary changes.

You may see old and new terms at the same time, such as “academic probation” and the newer “academic notice.”

Your situation can change which rule applies.

Major, catalog year, transfer destination, aid status, and local college policy can all matter. Ask when the stakes are high.

When a definition is not enough

Sometimes you need a person, not another webpage.

If the term affects registration, dropping a course, financial aid, academic standing, graduation, or transfer, confirm the rule for your own record.
